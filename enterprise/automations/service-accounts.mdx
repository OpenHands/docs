A service account is a non-human member of an organization. Automations can run as a service account instead of
as the person who created them. Owners and Admins create and manage service accounts.

<Note>
  Service accounts are in beta. A Super Admin turns them on for the installation. See
  [Turn On Service Accounts](#turn-on-service-accounts).
</Note>

## When to Use a Service Account

By default, an automation runs as the person who created it, with that person's connected Git accounts, secrets,
and budget. Use a service account when the automation belongs to the team rather than to one person:

- **Keep automations running when people leave.** When a person is removed from the organization, the automations
  that run as them stop. An automation that runs as a service account keeps running.
- **Use shared, dedicated credentials.** Give the service account its own secrets, such as a bot token with only
  the access the automation needs, instead of relying on a person's tokens.
- **Control who changes it.** Only Owners and Admins can edit, run, turn on, or delete an automation that runs as a
  service account. Members can view it, but can't change it, even if they created it.
- **Track and limit spend separately.** A service account can have its own monthly budget, and its runs are not
  billed to a person.
- **Stop a group of automations at once.** Disable the service account to stop every automation that runs as it,
  for example after a credential leak.

Service accounts do not take a license seat, can't sign in, and don't appear in the organization's member list.

## Turn On Service Accounts

A Super Admin turns on `Enable Service Accounts (beta)` on the `Instance Settings` page of the Super Admin
dashboard. The change takes effect without a redeploy.

## Create a Service Account

1. Go to `Settings` > `Service Accounts`.
2. Click `Create service account`.
3. Enter a name, such as `nightly-report`. Each name must be unique in the organization.

A service account belongs to one organization. Deleting the organization deletes its service accounts.

## Give a Service Account Credentials

A service account has no connected Git accounts. A run that uses it gets:

- The service account's own secrets.
- The organization's [shared secrets](/enterprise/secrets-management).
- The organization's LLM settings.

To add a secret, click `Secrets and budget` next to the service account, then click `Add secret`. For example,
add a `GITHUB_TOKEN` secret with a bot token so the automation can work with your repositories. Secret values are
write-only. You can replace or delete a value, but you can't view it after you save it. `OPENHANDS_API_KEY` is
reserved, because OpenHands adds it to every run.

## Set a Budget

When your installation uses the LiteLLM gateway, `Secrets and budget` also shows a `Monthly budget` section. Enter a
monthly limit in dollars to cap the service account's spend. Without a limit, the organization default applies.
Click `Remove limit` to go back to the default.

## Run an Automation as a Service Account

You must be an Owner or Admin.

- **New automation**: In the automation setup, choose the service account in the `Runs as` field. The default is
  `Me`.
- **Existing automation**: Open the automation and choose `Change runs as`. Pick a service account, or `Me` to run
  it as yourself.

When you change who an automation runs as:

- OpenHands turns the automation off. Turn it on again after you check that the new identity has the secrets the
  automation needs. Credentials do not move with the automation.
- The automation keeps its run history and stored data.
- You can't change a draft, or an automation with a pending or running run.

The automation's detail page shows `Automation Runs As` with the service account name and an `SA` badge, and
`Created by` with the person who created it. Members see `Service account` instead of the name.

An automation that runs as a service account uses plugins at a fixed commit. A push to the plugin's repository does
not change what the automation runs.

## Manage Service Accounts

On `Settings` > `Service Accounts`, use the buttons next to a service account to:

- **Rename** it.
- **Disable** it. This revokes its keys, and its automation runs fail until you enable it again. The list shows a
  `Disabled` label.
- **Enable** it again.
- **Delete** it. You can't undo this. OpenHands refuses to delete a service account while any automation runs as
  it. Change who those automations run as, or delete them, first.

A service account can't create automations, change who an automation runs as, or manage the automation it runs.
